## Research Note

Poor email deliverability silently kills SaaS activation rates — if your welcome email lands in spam, new tenants never complete onboarding and churn before they start.

- Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC DNS records for your sending domain. SPF whitelists your mail provider's IPs, DKIM cryptographically signs each email, and DMARC tells receiving servers what to do with failures. All three together are the baseline for inbox placement in 2024.
- Use a dedicated sending subdomain (`mail.platform.com`) separate from your main domain (`platform.com`). If your transactional emails ever get flagged, your main domain reputation is unaffected.
- Separate transactional emails (password reset, invite, notifications) from marketing emails (newsletters, feature announcements) using different IP pools or subaccounts in your ESP. Transactional emails must always reach the inbox — marketing emails can tolerate some filtering.
- Implement bounce and complaint webhooks from your ESP (SendGrid, Mailgun, AWS SES). Automatically suppress hard-bounced addresses and unsubscribe complaint senders immediately — continuing to send to these addresses tanks your sender reputation.
- Warm up new sending IPs gradually: start with 50 emails/day, double every 2 days over 4 weeks before sending at full volume.

Real-world example: Intercom improved their transactional email delivery rate from 94% to 99.2% after implementing DMARC enforcement and separating transactional from marketing IP pools.
