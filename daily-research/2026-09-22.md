## Research Note

For early‑stage SaaS targeting SMBs, a modular monolith typically delivers faster iteration and lower ops cost than a full microservice architecture, especially when time‑to‑market and budget constraints dominate.  
- Keep the codebase in a single repository with clear bounded contexts; use feature‑flags and layered packages to enforce separation, which speeds CI/CD cycles and reduces deployment friction.  
- Adopt a lightweight service‑mesh‑like library (e.g., gRPC‑gateway or HTTP adapters) only for inter‑module communication, so you can later extract services without rewriting business logic.  
- Allocate separate schemas or tenant‑id columns per customer in a shared database; enforce row‑level security to meet multi‑tenant isolation while avoiding the overhead of multiple databases.  
- Instrument granular health metrics (latency, error rate) per module and set automated scaling thresholds; this provides the observability needed for a future microservice split without incurring the cost of independent clusters now.  
These practices let you validate product‑market fit before committing to the operational complexity of independent services.  
Company X started with a modular monolith for its invoicing SaaS; after 12 months it processed 250 k invoices per month with 99.8 % uptime, and the initial development team grew from 3 to 7 engineers without adding any new infrastructure.
